The NASA Challenger Disaster

What was the purpose of the NASA Challenger mission?

The NASA Challenger rocket was designed to carry out a mission. This mission was to send the second Tracking and Data Relay System satellite into our orbit. This satellite could ensure near-continuous communication for later shuttle missions.

What went wrong?

The Challenger mission got off to a good start after the original mission was postponed. The ignition was going well and the rocket cleared the launch pad, but sadly approximately 73 seconds after lift off a fire, which was believed to have started from the booster, was increasingly growing bigger. It soon made it's way under the shuttle which turned the whole ship into one massive fireball. There was a big explosion followed by two smaller explosions in the sky. The smaller explosions were from the boosters which had detached from the main ship.

The first space teacher

The crew of the challenger consisted of five individuals, surprisingly one of them was a school teacher who underwent astronaut training for the mission. She wanted to make history by not only being the first teacher to go in to space but to also be the first person to teach a lesson from space. Sadly her class was watching the rocket live when the disaster occured where she tragically passed away.

What did NASA change after Challenger

There were many changes that NASA made to ensure subsequent launches were safer. For instance, they grounded shuttles for a few years while they corrected any faults, they designed and built a new orbiter with safety in mind, they created a new safety office, and they stopped using crewed shuttles for missions where they weren't necessary.
